Under review: the unit tasks' D1 and D3 interrupts

The unit tasks are the atomic tasks (no stages). Their interrupts of D1 (where the item is) and D3 (where the destination is) are under review, each in its three forms: continuous (a drift from the first tick), before (a slide as the hand nears the item) and during (a slide at the grasp's or the place's onset). Each entry links the variant's section and gives, per kitchen, how many of its levels are admitted (rule 6b, the admission decisions and the trigger gate applied). The control, D0 (something the task does not use drifts by the same amounts), is on each page; D4 of the pick-and-place tasks and D5 (the goal switches) are parked and folded at the end of each page; the fixture tasks' own interrupts (D4 of the A class, D8 of the S class) are in the table after this one.

The fixture tasks' interrupts: A (move a fixture, D4) and S (switches and knobs, D8)

A fixture task carries the interrupts that apply to it: the A class's own fixture drifts back against the goal or slides back during the opening (D4, no longer parked on these tasks), the S class's control (a faucet handle, a stove knob, a kettle lever) is turned back during the turn, before the success threshold (D8). A latched button has no joint to turn back and is the S class's control; D5 stays parked.

Repeats and chains: two events in one variant

A repeat is the same interrupt twice, a chain one interrupt and then another; the second waits for the first and for the policy to re-open the access part (the latch), so a policy that never re-opens never meets it. Read by the first event, the second's reach beside it; no post-event twin. Their previews set the access part back to its value at reset between the two events (the re-open preset).
